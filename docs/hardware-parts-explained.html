The 7 parts

1Raspberry Pi 5 + Active Cooler

A full computer the size of a deck of cards. Ours runs QNX instead of Linux.

In SafeStop: the brain. It reads the webcam, runs the AI hand detector, and sends GO or STOP to the Arduino.

Try this

  • Boot from the QNX card, then log in: ssh qnxuser@qnxpi.local (password qnxuser). Change it with passwd.
  • List AI packages: apk list --available | grep -i -E 'onnx|ncnn|tflite|opencv'.
  • Plug in the webcam and check that QNX sees it (generic USB camera, SENSOR_UNIT_5).
  • Quick win once you have an LED: blink it from Python with import rpi_gpio as GPIO.

Watch out

  • Use a 5 V 5 A USB-C supply. On a 3 A supply, all USB ports share only 600 mA. The webcam and Arduino may drop out.
  • Leave the fan cable plugged in. A hot Pi slows itself down, and the AI gets slower.
  • First boot finishes setting things up. Give it a few minutes before you pull power (QNX's headless setup guide says the same).
  • QNX recommends 8 GB of RAM. unverified how much this board has.

2HiPi.io case

A black box the Pi sits in. The open-case photo in #1 is probably this case with the lid off unverified.

In SafeStop: keeps the board safe from loose wires, spills and knocks. It also makes the demo look finished.

Try this

  • Keep the lid off while you build, so you can see the lights and reach the ports.
  • Put tape labels on the outside: "HDMI0", "camera (blue)", "Arduino".
  • Close it for judging.

Watch out

  • Check that the closed lid leaves room for the fan to breathe unverified fit.
  • The microSD slot is on the end opposite the USB ports. Check that you can still reach it.
  • Keep loose jumper wires and metal tools out of the case. They can short the board.

3"Runs on QNX" microSD card · SanDisk Ultra 32 GB

The Pi's hard drive. It holds QNX and every file you add.

In SafeStop: without it the Pi has nothing to start. Your detector code and model files live here.

Try this

  • Slide it into the slot under the Pi, gold contacts facing the board.
  • Set up Wi-Fi before boot: edit wpa_supplicant.conf on its boot partition through the card reader.
  • Once the demo works, copy the whole card to your laptop as an image file. A bad change then costs minutes, not hours.

Watch out

  • Shut down before you pull it out.
  • Re-flashing erases the card, your code included. Keep code in git on the laptop.
  • There is no spare. Keep it in its clear box when it is not in the Pi.

4USB microSD card reader

A USB stick with a microSD slot. It lets a laptop read and write the card.

In SafeStop: your repair tool. Use it to re-flash QNX or fix setup files when the Pi won't boot or won't join Wi-Fi.

Try this

  • Re-flash: in Raspberry Pi Imager pick Use custom, then qnx_sdp8.0_rpi5_quickstart_*.img.
  • Add your Wi-Fi to wpa_supplicant.conf on the boot partition.
  • Back up a working card as an image file.

Watch out

  • It has a USB-A plug. A MacBook with only USB-C ports needs an adapter.
  • Eject before you unplug, or your edits may not save.
  • If macOS offers to Initialize the card, click Ignore. Initializing erases QNX.

5Micro-HDMI to HDMI cable

A video cable. The small end goes in the Pi; the big end goes in a monitor or TV.

In SafeStop: lets you see the Pi's screen. It helps when SSH fails, and it can show a live view during the demo.

Try this

  • Plug the small end into HDMI0, the port next to the USB-C power port.
  • Connect the monitor before you power on.
  • If SSH can't find the Pi, plug in a monitor and USB keyboard, open the terminal from the QNX Welcome screen, log in as qnxuser / qnxuser, and run ifconfig.

Watch out

  • The two micro-HDMI ports look the same. With one screen, use HDMI0.
  • The Pi 5 uses micro-HDMI (not mini-HDMI), and this cable is the right type.
  • At boot the screen shows a splash, then the QNX Welcome screen with a terminal icon. Borrow a monitor from the hardware desk if you don't have one.

6Arduino Uno

A tiny computer with no operating system. It runs one program in a loop, with very steady timing.

In SafeStop: the reflexes. It reads the ultrasonic sensor, drives the relay, motor and buzzer, and stops the machine by itself if the Pi goes quiet.

Try this

  • Quick win: upload the Blink example from your laptop. The built-in LED (pin 13) blinks.
  • Upload the watchdog sketch. Test it from the Serial Monitor: type H to keep it running; stop typing and it stops after 200 ms.
  • Plug it into the Pi and check for /dev/serusb1 (usb -vvv).
  • Wire the ultrasonic sensor and print distances in the Serial Monitor.

Watch out

  • Opening the serial port resets the Uno. Open it once, wait about 2 s, then send. Keep it open.
  • A pin gives about 20 mA at 5 V. That is enough for an LED, not a motor. Use a relay or motor driver.
  • It needs a USB-A to USB-B ("printer") cable. We don't have one yet.
  • The port name /dev/serusb1 comes from the plan untested.

7Logitech 720p USB webcam

A USB camera. If it is a C270: 1280×720 video at up to 30 frames a second, fixed focus.

In SafeStop: the eyes. It sends frames to the Pi, and the AI looks for a hand near the machine.

Try this

  • Test it on your laptop first (Photo Booth) to frame the danger zone.
  • Mount it above the machine, looking down. Mark the danger zone with tape.
  • Plug it into a blue USB 3 port on the Pi.
  • Feed the AI small frames (for example 320×240) for a steadier frame rate.

Watch out

  • Fixed focus: very close hands blur. Test at your real demo distance.
  • QNX officially tests the Logitech C920. Ours should work the same way unverified.
  • Venue lighting changes. Test under the lights you will demo in.

Still need

USB-C power supply, 5 V 5 APowers the Pi (official 27 W). A cable in the Pi photo may be one unverified.
USB-A to USB-B cableConnects the Uno to the Pi, and to your laptop for uploads.
Ultrasonic sensorThe Arduino's own "too close" check (10 cm).
Relay or motor driver + motorThe "machine" that stops.
Buzzer and/or LEDShows the STOP state to judges.
E-stop buttonA human can always stop it.
Jumper wires (+ breadboard)Connect the sensor, relay and buzzer to the Uno.
Nice to haveMonitor, USB keyboard, USB-C adapter for the card reader.

Ask the hardware desk first. None of these appear in the photos.

  • Exact webcam model. The photos show Logitech and 720p only.
  • That the Uno appears as /dev/serusb1 on QNX, and whether QNX's USB serial driver resets it on open.
  • How much RAM this Pi 5 has (QNX recommends 8 GB).
  • Whether a USB-C power supply came with the kit.
  • Card reader model ("KH220") and HiPi.io case fit with the fan.
